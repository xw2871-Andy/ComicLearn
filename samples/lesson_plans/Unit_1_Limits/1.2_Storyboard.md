# [Unit 1.2] Limit of a Function and Limit Laws
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 2.2

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To understand the informal definition of a limit, the behavior of functions near a point (regardless of the point's value), and to master the Limit Laws for algebraic computation.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Informal Definition**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow c} f(x) = L$ means $f(x)$ is arbitrarily close to $L$ for all $x$ sufficiently close to $c$ (other than $c$ itself).
2. **Independence from $f(c)$**: The limit depends on behavior *near* $c$, not *at* $c$. (Example 2, Figure 2.8).
3. **Limit Laws (Theorem 1)**: Sum, Difference, Constant Multiple, Product, Quotient (Denominator $\neq 0$), Power, and Root Rules.
4. **Limits of Polynomials/Rational Functions**: Evaluated via direct substitution if the denominator is non-zero.
5. **Common Factors**: If $f(c) = 0/0$, factor out $(x-c)$ to evaluate (Example 7).
6. **Sandwich Theorem (Theorem 4)**: If $g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x)$ and $\lim g(x) = \lim h(x) = L$, then $\lim f(x) = L$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $f(x) = (x^2-1)/(x-1)$ has a "hole" at $x=1$, but $\lim_{x \rightarrow 1} f(x) = 2$.
- **Example 7**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow 1} (x^2+x-2)/(x^2-x) = \lim_{x \rightarrow 1} (x+2)/x = 3$.
- **Example 9**: Rationalizing the numerator: $\lim_{x \rightarrow 0} \frac{\sqrt{x^2+100}-10}{x^2} = 0.05$.
- **Example 10**: $1 - x^2/4 \leq u(x) \leq 1 + x^2/2 \implies \lim_{x \rightarrow 0} u(x) = 1$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Neighbor's Secret**

### Panel 1: The Invisible Bridge (Example 1)
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita approach a bridge where a single plank is missing at $x=1$. A sign says $f(x) = \frac{x^2-1}{x-1}$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! We can't cross! The function is undefined at $x=1$! There's a hole!"
  - **Doraemon**: "Don't panic, Nobita! In Calculus, we don't care about the hole. We only care about where the path is *leading* as we get closer and closer."

### Panel 2: The Limit Laws Gadget (Theorem 1)
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out the "Limit Distributor"—a machine with slots for Sum, Product, and Quotient. He feeds a complex equation into it.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Limit Laws let us break big problems into small ones. The limit of a sum is just the sum of the limits!"
  - **Nobita**: "It's like a sorting machine for math!"

### Panel 3: The Ghost Factor (Example 7)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at $f(x) = \frac{x^2+x-2}{x^2-x}$. At $x=1$, the "0/0 Monster" appears. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Oh no! Zero divided by zero! It's a disaster!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's just a 'ghost' factor hiding, Nobita! Use the 'Factoring Glasses'. $(x-1)$ is in both the top and bottom. Cancel them out!"

### Panel 4: The Conjugate Shield (Example 9)
- **Visual**: A square root function $\sqrt{x^2+100}-10$ is blocking the path. Nobita's calculator is showing $0.000...$ due to rounding errors (Example 8).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Computers lie, Nobita! They can't handle the tiny details. Use the 'Conjugate Shield' ($\sqrt{x^2+100}+10$) to rationalize the numerator!"
  - **Nobita**: "Wow! The $x^2$ cancels out, and the limit is exactly $0.05$!"

### Panel 5: The Sandwich Trap (Theorem 4)
- **Visual**: A wild, oscillating function $u(x)$ is trapped between two smooth parabolas (Figure 2.13). The parabolas meet at $y=1$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "This function $u(x)$ is too crazy to calculate directly. But look! It's 'sandwiched' between two reliable friends. Since they both go to 1, it has no choice but to go to 1 too!"
  - **Nobita**: "The Squeeze Theorem! It's like a mathematical trap!"

### Panel 6: The Continuity Preview
- **Visual**: Doraemon points to three different graphs (Figure 2.8). One has a hole, one has a jump, and one is a solid line.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If the limit equals the function value, the path is smooth. We call that 'Continuity'. But that's a story for another day!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus is starting to make sense!"
