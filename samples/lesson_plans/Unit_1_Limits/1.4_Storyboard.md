# [Unit 1.4] One-Sided Limits
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 2.4

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To extend the limit concept to cases where approach is restricted to one side, particularly at points of discontinuity or interval endpoints, and to master the special trigonometric limit $\lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{\sin \theta}{\theta} = 1$.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **One-Sided Limits**: $\lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = L$ (Right-hand) and $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = M$ (Left-hand).
2. **Theorem 6**: $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L \iff \lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = L \text{ and } \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = L$.
3. **Trig Limit Theorem (Theorem 7)**: $\lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{\sin \theta}{\theta} = 1$.
4. **Sandwich Proof Strategy**: Proving the trig limit using area inequalities: $\frac{1}{2} \sin \theta < \frac{\theta}{2} < \frac{1}{2} \tan \theta$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Semicircle $f(x) = \sqrt{4-x^2}$ has limit 0 at $x=2$ (left-hand) and $x=-2$ (right-hand).
- **Example 2**: A piecewise function (Figure 2.27) illustrating where limits exist vs. where one-sided limits differ.
- **Example 4**: $y = \sin(1/x)$ fails to have even one-sided limits at $x=0$ due to oscillation.
- **Example 5b**: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{5x} = \frac{2}{5} (1) = \frac{2}{5}$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The One-Way Street**

### Panel 1: The Broken Digital Bridge (Figure 2.24)
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita at a digital gap. From the left, the path is at $y=-1$. From the right, it's at $y=1$. This is the $f(x) = x/|x|$ graph.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! Depending on which side I walk from, I end up in a different place!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's a Jump Discontinuity, Nobita! We must specify the direction: $x \to 0^-$ from the left, and $x \to 0^+$ from the right."

### Panel 2: The Meeting Theorem (Theorem 6)
- **Visual**: Two versions of Nobita (Left-Nobita and Right-Nobita) are trying to high-five. If they are at different heights, they miss. If they are at the same height, the "True Limit" glows.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The 'Full' Limit only exists if the left hand and the right hand meet at the same value $L$. If they don't agree, the limit is 'Does Not Exist'!"

### Panel 3: The Edge of the World (Example 1)
- **Visual**: Doraemon at the boundary of a semicircle $x=2$. There is no bridge to the right. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "At a boundary point, the limit is simply the one-sided limit that is defined. For this semicircle, the limit at $x=2$ is just the left-hand limit: zero!"

### Panel 4: The Squeeze Circle (Theorem 7 Proof)
- **Visual**: A unit circle $OAP$ with a sector and two triangles. Areas are labeled with inequalities. Doraemon is shrinking the angle $\theta$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Now for a classic! We 'sandwich' the area of this sector between two triangles. As $\theta$ goes to zero, they squeeze $\sin \theta / \theta$ until it has no choice but to be exactly 1!"

### Panel 5: The Trig Puzzle (Example 5b)
- **Visual**: Holographic blackboard showing $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{5x}$. Doraemon is pulling a "2/5" fraction out as a constant.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "To use our new theorem, we just need the 'insides' to match! Multiply by $2/2$ and rearrange. The answer is two-fifths!"

### Panel 6: The Infinite Oscillation (Example 4)
- **Visual**: A wild, zig-zagging graph of $y = \sin(1/x)$ near $x=0$. It's a blur of lines.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "This one is too crazy! It's not even going to a single number on one side!"
  - **Doraemon**: "Correct! That's oscillation. Some functions are just too unstable to have any limit at all!"
