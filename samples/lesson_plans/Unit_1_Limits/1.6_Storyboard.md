# [Unit 1.6] Limits Involving Infinity; Asymptotes of Graphs
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 2.6

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To analyze function behavior as $x$ approaches $\pm \infty$ (horizontal asymptotes) and as $f(x)$ grows without bound (vertical asymptotes), and to master the concept of dominant terms.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Finite Limits at Infinity**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow \pm \infty} f(x) = L$ means $f(x)$ gets arbitrarily close to $L$ as $x$ grows large.
2. **Horizontal Asymptote**: The line $y=b$ is a horizontal asymptote if $\lim_{x \rightarrow \infty} f(x) = b$ or $\lim_{x \rightarrow -\infty} f(x) = b$.
3. **Infinite Limits**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow c} f(x) = \infty$ describes behavior where values outgrow all finite bounds.
4. **Vertical Asymptote**: The line $x=a$ is a vertical asymptote if $\lim_{x \rightarrow a^\pm} f(x) = \pm \infty$.
5. **Oblique (Slant) Asymptote**: If the degree of the numerator is 1 greater than the denominator, the graph approaches a slanted line (linear part + remainder).
6. **Dominant Terms**: For large $x$, a polynomial behaves like its highest-degree term (Example 18).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 3a**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{5x^2+8x-3}{3x^2+2} = 5/3$.
- **Example 5b**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow \infty} x \sin(1/x) = \lim_{t \rightarrow 0^+} \frac{\sin t}{t} = 1$.
- **Example 9**: $f(x) = \frac{x^2-3}{2x-4} = (\frac{x}{2}+1) + \frac{1}{2x-4}$ has a slant asymptote $y = x/2 + 1$.
- **Example 11**: $\lim_{x \rightarrow 0} \frac{1}{x^2} = \infty$ (Consistent behavior from both sides).

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Edge of Infinity**

### Panel 1: The Endless Road (Limits at Infinity)
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita are on a road that stretches to the horizon. A sign says "$x \to \infty$". A holographic graph of $y = 1/x$ shows the path getting closer and closer to the ground ($y=0$).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon, how far does this road go? Will it ever touch the ground?"
  - **Doraemon**: "It gets arbitrarily close, Nobita! As $x$ goes to infinity, $1/x$ goes to zero. That ground level is our Horizontal Asymptote!"

### Panel 2: The Ratio Race (Example 3a)
- **Visual**: Two functions are racing: $5x^2$ and $3x^2$. The smaller terms ($8x, -3, 2$) are falling off like dead weight.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "When $x$ is huge, only the biggest powers matter! For $\frac{5x^2+8x-3}{3x^2+2}$, the $x^2$ terms dominate. The race ends in a tie at $5/3$!"

### Panel 3: The Infinite Spike (Vertical Asymptotes)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at a "Gravity Well" graph of $y = 1/x^2$. As he walks toward $x=0$, the path shoots straight up into space.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Whoa! This path doesn't just jump, it launches! I can't even see the top!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's an Infinite Limit! The line $x=0$ is a Vertical Asymptote. The function outgrows all finite bounds!"

### Panel 4: The Slant Slide (Example 9)
- **Visual**: A playground slide that isn't horizontal or vertical, but slanted. The slide formula is $f(x) = \frac{x^2-3}{2x-4}$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "When the top is one degree higher than the bottom, we get a Slant Asymptote! Using 'Polynomial Division', we see this slide eventually follows the line $y = x/2 + 1$."

### Panel 5: The Trig Infinity Trick (Example 5b)
- **Visual**: Doraemon uses the "Reciprocal Lens" ($t = 1/x$) on the function $x \sin(1/x)$ as $x \to \infty$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Infinity is hard to see. But if we let $t = 1/x$, then as $x$ goes to infinity, $t$ goes to zero! Now it's just our old friend $\sin(t)/t$."
  - **Nobita**: "And we know that limit is 1! Magic!"

### Panel 6: The Dominant Leader (Example 18)
- **Visual**: A giant robot $f(x) = 3x^4 - 2x^3 + \dots$ standing next to a smaller robot $g(x) = 3x^4$. Far away, they look identical. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "On a small scale, every term counts. But on the scale of the universe, the leader ($3x^4$) is all anyone sees. They behave like twins at infinity!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus helps us see the big picture!"
