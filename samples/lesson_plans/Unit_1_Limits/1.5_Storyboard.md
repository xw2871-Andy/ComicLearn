# [Unit 1.5] Continuity
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 2.5

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To define continuity at a point and over an interval, identify types of discontinuities, and apply the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) for root finding and connectivity.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Continuity Test**: A function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x=c$ iff:
   - $f(c)$ exists.
   - $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists.
   - $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.
2. **Discontinuity Types**:
   - **Removable**: Limit exists, but $f(c)$ is undefined or doesn't match (e.g., $f(x)=(x^2-1)/(x-1)$).
   - **Jump**: One-sided limits exist but are different (e.g., Unit Step Function $U(x)$).
   - **Infinite**: Function grows without bound (e.g., $1/x^2$).
   - **Oscillating**: Function fluctuates too much to have a limit (e.g., $\sin(1/x)$).
3. **Continuous Extension**: Redefining a function at a removable discontinuity to make it continuous (e.g., $(\sin x)/x$ with $f(0)=1$).
4. **Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)**: If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, it takes on every value between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Analysis of a piecewise graph (Figure 2.35) with jumps at $x=1$ and removable holes at $x=2, 4$.
- **Example 10**: Proving $x^3-x-1=0$ has a root in $[1, 2]$ because $f(1)=-1$ and $f(2)=5$.
- **Example 12**: Continuous extension of $f(x) = (x^2+x-6)/(x^2-4)$ to $x=2$ is $F(2)=5/4$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Unbroken Path**

### Panel 1: The Sketching Rule (Intuition)
- **Visual**: Doraemon is sketching a smooth curve on a canvas in one unbroken motion. Nobita is trying to do the same but keeps lifting his pen.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "A continuous function is like a perfect sketch, Nobita. If you can draw it without lifting your pen from the paper, it's likely continuous!"
  - **Nobita**: "But what if there's a tiny gap or a sudden jump?"

### Panel 2: The Continuity Test (The Three Conditions)
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out a "Continuity Scanner" with three glowing check-boxes: 1. Defined? 2. Limit Exists? 3. Match?
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "To pass the test at point $c$, the function must be defined, the limit must exist, and they must be exactly the same! If even one box is empty, the path is broken."

### Panel 3: The Hall of Discontinuities (Figure 2.40)
- **Visual**: A gallery of "Broken Paths". A "Removable Hole" ($1/x^2$), a "Jump" (Step function), and an "Infinite Spike" ($1/x$).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Look at these, Nobita! The 'Jump' is where the left and right sides don't meet. The 'Removable' is just a missing pixel we can fix!"
  - **Nobita**: "And the 'Infinite' one goes straight to the moon!"

### Panel 4: The Continuous Extension (Example 12)
- **Visual**: Doraemon is using a "Pixel Filler" gadget on a graph with a hole at $x=2$. The formula $(x^2+x-6)/(x^2-4)$ is being simplified to $(x+3)/(x+2)$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "We can't plug in 2, but the limit is $5/4$. By defining the value at the hole as $1.25$, we've extended the function and healed the bridge!"

### Panel 5: The Intermediate Value Mountain (Theorem 11)
- **Visual**: A mountain path from height -1 to height 5. A horizontal line at height 0 (The X-Axis) crosses the path. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If you start in a valley ($y=-1$) and end on a peak ($y=5$), and the path is continuous, you *must* have crossed sea level ($y=0$) at some point!"
  - **Nobita**: "That's the IVT! It guarantees there's a root hidden somewhere in between!"

### Panel 6: The Connected World
- **Visual**: A summary screen showing the "Connectedness" of functions like polynomials and sin/cos.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Most of our favorite functions are continuous everywhere they are defined. They are the reliable backbones of everything we build in Calculus!"
  - **Nobita**: "No jumps, no surprises. I like that!"
