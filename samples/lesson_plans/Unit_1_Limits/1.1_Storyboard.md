# [Unit 1.1] Rates of Change and Tangent Lines
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 2.1

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To introduce the conceptual transition from average rates of change (Algebra) to instantaneous rates of change (Calculus) through the geometry of secant and tangent lines.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Galileo's Free Fall Law**: $y = 16t^2$ ft (distance fallen after $t$ seconds).
2. **Average Speed**: Distance traveled divided by elapsed time: $\frac{f(t_1+h) - f(t_1)}{h}$.
3. **Average Rate of Change**: The slope of the secant line through $P(x_1, f(x_1))$ and $Q(x_2, f(x_2))$.
4. **Instantaneous Rate of Change**: The limiting value of the average rate of change as $h \to 0$ (the slope of the tangent line).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Rock Fall**: Average speed over [1, 2] is $48$ ft/sec. Instantaneous speed at $t=1$ is $32$ ft/sec.
- **Fruit Fly Growth**: Average rate from Day 23 to 45 is $\approx 8.6$ flies/day. Instantaneous rate on Day 23 is $\approx 16.7$ flies/day.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Mystery of the Instant**

### Panel 1: Galileo's Cliff Challenge
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita stand on a tall cliff. Doraemon drops a rock. A holographic display shows $y = 16t^2$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Nobita! Galileo found that falling objects follow $y = 16t^2$. Let's track its speed!"
  - **Nobita**: "Easy! At 1 second it's at 16 feet, at 2 seconds it's at 64 feet. So it averaged 48 feet per second!"

### Panel 2: The 0/0 Dilemma
- **Visual**: Nobita holding a stopwatch, looking puzzled as the rock zooms past.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "But how fast was it going at *exactly* 1 second? If I use zero time, my calculator says Error!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's the Algebra Wall, Nobita! To see the 'instant', we have to look at what happens as the time interval shrinks to nothing."

### Panel 3: The Secant Slider (Geometric)
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out a "Secant Slider" gadget—a large graph of $y = x^2$ with two points, P and Q, connected by a line (Secant).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Point P is stuck at 2 seconds. Watch as I slide Point Q closer and closer to P."
  - **Nobita**: "The line is spinning! It's starting to just graze the curve at P!"

### Panel 4: The Tangent Emerges (Thomas Example 3)
- **Visual**: Close-up on Point $P(2, 4)$. The line has become a glowing red Tangent Line. Mathematical text: $\text{Slope} = \lim_{h \to 0} (4 + h) = 4$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The limit of those secant slopes is exactly 4. That is the slope of the curve at P!"
  - **Nobita**: "So the Tangent Line is like the 'shadow' of the curve's direction at that one point!"

### Panel 5: Fruit Fly Population (Example 4/5)
- **Visual**: A lab setting with fruit flies. Figure 2.5 is projected: a population curve showing $16.7$ flies/day at Day 23.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Calculus isn't just for rocks, Nobita. It tracks biology too! The average growth was 8.6, but the instantaneous growth at Day 23 was 16.7 flies per day!"

### Panel 6: The Gateway to Limits
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita standing before a glowing gate labeled "THE LIMIT".
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "To make this 'instant' precise, we need to master the tool that bridges Algebra and Calculus. Are you ready for the Limit?"
  - **Nobita**: "Let's do it, Doraemon!"
