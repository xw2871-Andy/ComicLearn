# [Unit 2.1] Tangent Lines and the Derivative at a Point
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.1

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To formally define the derivative at a point as the limit of the difference quotient, and to understand its multi-faceted interpretations as slope and rate of change.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Difference Quotient**: $\frac{f(x_0+h) - f(x_0)}{h}$.
2. **Derivative at a Point**: $f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0+h) - f(x_0)}{h}$.
3. **Interpretations of the Derivative**:
   - The slope of the graph of $y=f(x)$ at $x=x_0$.
   - The slope of the tangent line to the curve at $x=x_0$.
   - The instantaneous rate of change of $f(x)$ with respect to $x$ at $x=x_0$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: For $y=1/x$, the slope at any point $a$ is $-1/a^2$. 
  - At $x=-1$, slope is $-1$.
  - Slope equals $-1/4$ at $x=2$ and $x=-2$.
- **Example 2**: For $f(t)=16t^2$ (Free fall), the exact speed at $t=1$ is $f'(1) = 32 \text{ ft/sec}$.
