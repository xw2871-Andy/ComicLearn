# [Unit 2.6] The Chain Rule
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.6

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To differentiate composite functions using the Chain Rule and the Power Chain Rule, and to understand the conceptual model of chained rates of change using the gear train analogy.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **The Chain Rule**: If $y=f(u)$ and $u=g(x)$, then $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$.
2. **"Outside-Inside" Rule**: Differentiate the outside function $f$, leaving the inside $g(x)$ alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
3. **Power Chain Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(u^n) = nu^{n-1} \frac{du}{dx}$.
4. **Gear Train Analogy**: Rates of change multiply like connected gears. If Gear B turns 3 times for A's 1, and C turns 1/2 for B's 1, then C turns 3/2 times for A's 1.
5. **Absolute Value Derivative**: $\frac{d}{dx}|x| = \frac{x}{|x|}, x \neq 0$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $y = (3x^2+1)^2 \implies y' = 2(3x^2+1) \cdot 6x = 36x^3 + 12x$.
- **Example 2**: $x(t) = \cos(t^2+1) \implies v(t) = -\sin(t^2+1) \cdot 2t$.
- **Example 4 (Repeated)**: $g(t) = \tan(5-\sin 2t) \implies g'(t) = \sec^2(5-\sin 2t) \cdot (-\cos 2t) \cdot 2$.
- **Example 8**: $\frac{d}{dx} \sin(x^\circ) = \frac{\pi}{180} \cos(x^\circ)$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Magic Gear Train**

### Panel 1: The Gear Gadget (Figure 3.24)
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out a "Gear Train" gadget with three connected gears: A, B, and C. A is linked to Nobita's input $x$. C is the output $y$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The Chain Rule is like these gears, Nobita! If gear B spins 3 times faster than A, and C spins half as fast as B, then C spins 1.5 times faster than A. Rates of change just multiply!"

### Panel 2: Peeling the Onion (Outside-Inside)
- **Visual**: Doraemon is peeling a mathematical onion. The outer layer is $\sin(u)$ and the inner layer is $(x^2-4)$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "To differentiate $\sin(x^2-4)$, we peel it layer by layer. Differentiate the Outside (Sine becomes Cosine), keep the Inside, then multiply by the derivative of the Inside ($2x$)!"

### Panel 3: The Power Chain (Example 1)
- **Visual**: A heavy box labeled $(3x^2+1)^2$. Doraemon uses a crane (The Power Chain Rule) to lift it. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The power '2' comes down like a crane hook, but don't forget to multiply by the 'engine' inside: the $6x$!"
  - **Nobita**: "So it's $2 \times (3x^2+1) \times 6x$. That's much easier than expanding everything!"

### Panel 4: The Double Chain (Example 4)
- **Visual**: A "Matryoshka Doll" of functions: $\tan( \dots )$, then $(5-\sin( \dots ))$, then $(2t)$. Doraemon opens them one by one.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Sometimes the chain has many links! We differentiate the Tangent, then the Sine, then the $2t$. Just keep multiplying until you reach the center!"

### Panel 5: The Degree Trap (Example 8)
- **Visual**: A sign warns "RADIANS ONLY". Nobita is trying to differentiate $\sin(x^\circ)$. A tiny $\pi/180$ factor pops out like a Jack-in-the-box.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Be careful with degrees, Nobita! Since one degree is $\pi/180$ radians, the Chain Rule forces that tiny constant out. That's why we love radians in Calculus!"

### Panel 6: The Absolute Truth (Example 6)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at $|x|$. Doraemon shows him that $|x|$ is just $\sqrt{x^2}$. He uses the Power Chain Rule on the square root.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Even the absolute value follows the chain! Its derivative is just $x/|x|$. It's $+1$ for positive numbers and $-1$ for negative ones. Logical, right?"
  - **Nobita**: "The Chain Rule connects everything!"
