# [Unit 2.2] The Derivative as a Function
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.2

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To understand the derivative as a function itself ($f'$), master different notations, and identify where a function fails to be differentiable, including the crucial relationship between differentiability and continuity.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **The Derivative Function**: $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ (if the limit exists).
2. **Notation**: $f'(x) = y' = \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{df}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}f(x)$.
3. **Calculating Derivatives**:
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\frac{1}{x}) = -\frac{1}{x^2}$.
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{x}) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}, x > 0$.
4. **Where Differentiability Fails**:
   - **Corner**: One-sided derivatives differ (e.g., $|x|$ at $x=0$).
   - **Cusp**: Slope approaches $\pm \infty$.
   - **Vertical Tangent**: Slope approaches $\infty$ from both sides.
   - **Discontinuity**: If $f$ is not continuous, it cannot be differentiable.
5. **Theorem 1**: Differentiability $\implies$ Continuity. (The converse is false: Continuity $\not\implies$ Differentiability).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $f(x) = \frac{x}{x-1} \implies f'(x) = \frac{-1}{(x-1)^2}$.
- **Example 4**: $|x|$ has right-hand derivative $+1$ and left-hand derivative $-1$ at $x=0$.
- **Example 5**: $\sqrt{x}$ has a vertical tangent at $x=0$ (derivative is $\infty$).

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Function of Change**

### Panel 1: The Evolution (Derivative as Function)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows a standard function $f(x)$ on one screen and a second, glowing screen showing $f'(x)$. As a point moves along $f(x)$, the second screen tracks its slope.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The Derivative isn't just a number at one point, Nobita. It's a whole new function derived from the original! It tells us the slope *anywhere* we want!"

### Panel 2: The Notation Shop (Newton vs. Leibniz)
- **Visual**: A store with two sections. Newton's section has "Primes" ($f', y'$). Leibniz's section has "Differentials" ($\frac{dy}{dx}, \frac{d}{dx}$).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Calculus has two languages, Nobita. Newton liked the Prime notation, but Leibniz's $\frac{dy}{dx}$ reminds us that it's all about the 'change in $y$ over change in $x$'."

### Panel 3: The Rational Robot (Example 1)
- **Visual**: A robot named "Rational-Bot" $f(x) = \frac{x}{x-1}$. Doraemon uses the Difference Quotient on it, simplifying the fractions.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Differentiating rational functions takes patience! See? The $h$ cancels out, leaving us with a beautiful new formula for the slope: $-1/(x-1)^2$."

### Panel 4: The Corner Collision (Example 4)
- **Visual**: Nobita is running along the "V" shape of $y = |x|$. At the bottom $(0,0)$, he hits a sharp corner and trips.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Ouch! This path is too sharp! My slope meter can't decide if it's $-1$ or $+1$!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's a Corner, Nobita! Since the left and right derivatives don't match, the function is NOT differentiable here."

### Panel 5: The Vertical Launch (Example 5)
- **Visual**: A graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$ starting at the origin. At $x=0$, the path starts straight up.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "At the very start, $\sqrt{x}$ is vertical. Since the slope is infinity, the derivative doesn't exist at zero. It's too steep for Calculus!"

### Panel 6: The "One-Way" Theorem (Theorem 1)
- **Visual**: A one-way sign: "Differentiable $\implies$ Continuous". A warning sign: "Continuous $\not\implies$ Differentiable".
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Every differentiable function is continuous. But just because a path is unbroken doesn't mean it's smooth enough to have a derivative! Remember the Corner!"
  - **Nobita**: "Smoothness is the key!"
