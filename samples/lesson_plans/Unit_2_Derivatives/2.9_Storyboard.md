# [Unit 2.9] Linearization and Differentials
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.9

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To use the tangent line as a local linear approximation for complicated functions (Linearization) and to introduce Differentials ($dx, dy$) for estimating measurement errors.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Linearization**: $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a)$.
2. **Standard Linear Approximation**: $f(x) \approx L(x)$ for $x$ near $a$.
3. **Differentials**: $dy = f'(x) dx$. $dx$ is an independent variable, $dy$ is dependent.
4. **Geometric Meaning**: $dy$ is the change in the *linearization* ($\Delta L$), while $\Delta y$ is the *true change* in the function.
5. **Sensitivity to Change**: Describing change as absolute ($\Delta f$), relative ($\Delta f/f$), or percentage ($\Delta f/f \times 100$).
6. **Famous Approximation**: $(1+x)^k \approx 1 + kx$ (for $x$ near 0).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Linearization of $\sqrt{1+x}$ at $a=0$ is $L(x) = 1 + x/2$.
- **Example 6 (Circle)**: Radius increases from 10m to 10.1m. Estimated area increase $dA = 2\pi(10)(0.1) = 2\pi$ m$^2$.
- **Example 9 (Einstein)**: Using $1/\sqrt{1-x^2} \approx 1 + x^2/2$ to show $(\Delta m)c^2 \approx \Delta(KE)$, linking mass and energy.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Magic Straightener**

### Panel 1: The Magnifying Glass (Linearization)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at a complex curve. Doraemon pulls out a "Super Magnifying Glass". As they zoom in on point $a$, the curve looks flatter and flatter until it looks like a straight line.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Every smooth curve is secretly straight if you look closely enough, Nobita! This straight line is the Linearization $L(x)$. It's much easier to work with than the original function!"

### Panel 2: The Square Root Hack (Example 1)
- **Visual**: Nobita is struggling to calculate $\sqrt{1.005}$. Doraemon shows him the formula $L(x) = 1 + x/2$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Instead of a calculator, use the Linearization at zero! For $\sqrt{1+x}$, just add half of $x$ to 1. So $\sqrt{1.005}$ is about $1.0025$. Fast and accurate!"

### Panel 3: The Target Center (Example 2)
- **Visual**: A graph of $\sqrt{1+x}$ with two straight lines touching it: one at $x=0$ and one at $x=3$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "A linearization is only good near its center, Nobita. If you move from $x=0$ to $x=3$, your old line becomes a 'crude' liar. You need a new center for a new truth!"

### Panel 4: True Change vs. Differential (Figure 3.44)
- **Visual**: A mountain path (Function) and a ramp (Tangent Line). Doraemon marks the vertical gap between the ramp and the path as the "Error".
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The true change $\Delta y$ follows the path, but the Differential $dy$ follows the ramp. As long as our step $dx$ is small, the ramp is a great shortcut!"

### Panel 5: The Circle Expansion (Example 6)
- **Visual**: A circle of radius 10m growing a tiny bit to 10.1m. Doraemon highlights the thin outer ring.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "How much did the area grow? Using differentials, $dA = 2\pi r dr$. It's about $2\pi$ square meters. We can estimate growth without recalculating the whole circle!"

### Panel 6: Einstein's Secret (Example 9)
- **Visual**: A rocket zooming near the speed of light. Mathematical formulas float in space: $m \approx m_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_0(v/c)^2$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Even Einstein used Linearization, Nobita! By approximating the mass formula, he showed how kinetic energy and mass are linked. Calculus is the key to $E=mc^2$!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus really is the language of the universe!"
