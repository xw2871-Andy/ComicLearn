# [Unit 2.4] The Derivative as a Rate of Change
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.4

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To apply the derivative to real-world modeling of change, specifically in physics (motion along a line), economics (marginal analysis), and biology (sensitivity to change).

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Instantaneous Rate of Change**: The derivative $f'(x_0)$ is the limit of the average rate of change.
2. **Motion Along a Line**:
   - **Velocity**: $v(t) = ds/dt$.
   - **Speed**: $|v(t)|$.
   - **Acceleration**: $a(t) = dv/dt = d^2s/dt^2$.
   - **Jerk**: $j(t) = da/dt = d^3s/dt^3$.
3. **Free Fall**: $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ ($g=32$ ft/sec$^2$ or $9.8$ m/sec$^2$).
4. **Economics**:
   - **Marginal Cost**: $dc/dx$ (approx. the cost of 1 additional unit).
5. **Sensitivity to Change**: $|f'(x)|$ measures how sensitive a function is to small changes in input (e.g., Mendel's pea genetics).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 4 (Dynamite Rock)**: Launch velocity 160 ft/sec. $s = 160t - 16t^2$. Max height (where $v=0$) is 400 ft at $t=5$. Acceleration is constant $-32$ ft/sec$^2$.
- **Example 5 (Radiators)**: Cost $c(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 15x$. Marginal cost at $x=10$ is $c'(10) = \$195$.
- **Example 7 (Mendel's Peas)**: $y = 2p - p^2$. Sensitivity $dy/dp = 2-2p$. Most sensitive when $p$ is small.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Physics of the Rocket**

### Panel 1: The Rocket Launch (Example 4)
- **Visual**: Nobita has a water rocket. Doraemon uses a dynamite blast to launch a rock straight up. A holographic display shows $s = 160t - 16t^2$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Whoa! It's going so fast! How high will it go, Doraemon?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Just watch the velocity, Nobita! When the rocket stops for an instant at the top, its velocity $v$ is zero!"

### Panel 2: The Speed vs. Velocity (Direction)
- **Visual**: The rock is falling back down. A speedometer shows "96 ft/sec". A velocity meter shows "-96 ft/sec".
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Wait, why is the velocity negative but the speed is positive?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Velocity tells us direction, Nobita! Positive is up, negative is down. But speed is just the absolute value—it doesn't care which way you're going!"

### Panel 3: The Acceleration & Jerk
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows a graph of the rock's motion. The acceleration is a flat line at $-32$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "On Earth, gravity pulls everything down at a constant 32 feet per second squared. Since the acceleration is constant, there's zero 'Jerk'—the ride is perfectly smooth!"

### Panel 4: The Radiator Factory (Example 5)
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita are in a small factory making radiators. A computer screen shows the "Marginal Cost" meter.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "In business, we use derivatives too! If we want to know the cost of making *one more* radiator, we look at the Marginal Cost $c'(x)$."
  - **Nobita**: "At 10 radiators, the next one costs 195 dollars!"

### Panel 5: Mendel's Pea Garden (Example 7)
- **Visual**: Doraemon is in a garden with smooth and wrinkled peas. He's holding a magnifying glass over a small frequency $p$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Even biology is sensitive! Mendel's peas follow $y = 2p - p^2$. The derivative $2-2p$ shows that when smooth genes are rare, just a few more change the whole population!"

### Panel 6: The Universal Tool
- **Visual**: A montage of the rocket, the factory, and the garden. The derivative symbol $d/dx$ glows over all of them.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Velocity, Marginal Cost, Genetic Sensitivity... they are all just rates of change. The Derivative is the universal language of how the world moves!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus is everywhere!"
