# [Unit 2.3] Differentiation Rules
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.3

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To master the fundamental rules of differentiation—including the Power, Sum, Difference, Constant Multiple, Product, and Quotient Rules—and to compute higher-order derivatives.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Constant Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$.
2. **Power Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$ (valid for any real number $n$).
3. **Constant Multiple Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(cu) = c \frac{du}{dx}$.
4. **Sum/Difference Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(u \pm v) = \frac{du}{dx} \pm \frac{dv}{dx}$.
5. **Product Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = u \frac{dv}{dx} + v \frac{du}{dx}$.
6. **Quotient Rule**: $\frac{d}{dx}(\frac{u}{v}) = \frac{v \frac{du}{dx} - u \frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$.
7. **Higher-Order Derivatives**: $y'', y''', y^{(n)}$ are derivatives of derivatives.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $\frac{d}{dx}(x^{2/3}) = \frac{2}{3}x^{-1/3}$; $\frac{d}{dx}(x^{-4}) = -4x^{-5}$.
- **Example 4**: For $y = x^4 - 2x^2 + 2$, horizontal tangents ($y' = 0$) occur at $x = 0, 1, -1$.
- **Example 5**: For $y = (x^2+1)(x^3+3)$, $y' = 5x^4 + 3x^2 + 6x$.
- **Example 8**: For $y = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2$, $y' = 3x^2 - 6x$, $y'' = 6x-6$, $y''' = 6$, $y^{(4)} = 0$.

**Topic: The Differentiation Shortcuts**

### Panel 1: The Rule Book (The Power Rule)
- **Visual**: Doraemon hands Nobita a "Secret Technique Scroll" (Power Rule). On it, the formula $x^n \to nx^{n-1}$ is glowing. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Nobita! No more long limits! If you see an exponent, just bring it down in front and subtract one from the power!"
  - **Nobita**: "So $x^3$ becomes $3x^2$? That's way faster!"

### Panel 2: The Rescaling Gadget (Constant Multiple)
- **Visual**: A graph of $y = x^2$ being stretched into $y = 3x^2$ (Figure 3.11). 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If you multiply the function by 3, you just multiply the slope by 3! Constants are easy, they just ride along for the derivative!"

### Panel 3: The Tangent Hunter (Example 4)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking for flat spots on the graph of $x^4 - 2x^2 + 2$. Doraemon points to the peaks and valleys.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Horizontal tangents mean the slope is zero. We just set the derivative $4x^3 - 4x$ to zero and solve! Look, three locations found: $0, 1$, and $-1$!"

### Panel 4: The Area expansion (Product Rule)
- **Visual**: An expanding rectangle with sides $u$ and $v$ (Picturing the Product Rule). Red-shaded areas represent the change.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The change in a product isn't just $u' \times v'$. It's the sum of two changes: $uv' + vu'$! Think of it like an expanding garden!"

### Panel 5: The High-Low mnemonic (Quotient Rule)
- **Visual**: A holographic fraction $u/v$. Doraemon is chanting a rhyme to help Nobita remember: "Low d-High minus High d-Low, over Low-squared!"
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Wait, $(\text{Low} \cdot u' - \text{High} \cdot v') / v^2$? This one is a mouthful, but it works!"

### Panel 6: The Derivative Tower (Higher-Order)
- **Visual**: A tower of functions. On the bottom floor is $y$, then $y'$, then $y''$. By the fourth floor ($y^{(4)}$), the function for a polynomial becomes zero.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "We can keep differentiating forever! The second derivative $y''$ tracks how the slope itself is changing. But for polynomials, eventually, you run out of power and hit zero!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus is like an onion—it has so many layers!"
