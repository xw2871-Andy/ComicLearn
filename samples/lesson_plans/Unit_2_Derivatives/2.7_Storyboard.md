# [Unit 2.7] Implicit Differentiation
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.7

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To differentiate functions that are not defined explicitly (as $y=f(x)$) but implicitly (as $F(x, y)=0$), and to find equations for tangent and normal lines to such curves.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Implicit Relation**: An equation where $y$ is not isolated (e.g., $x^2+y^2=25$ or $x^3+y^3-9xy=0$).
2. **The Technique**:
   - Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.
   - Treat $y$ as a differentiable function of $x$ (apply the Chain Rule: $\frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = 2y \frac{dy}{dx}$).
   - Collect $dy/dx$ terms and solve.
3. **Normal Line**: The line perpendicular to the tangent line at a point (slope is $-1/m$).
4. **Folium of Descartes**: The curve $x^3+y^3-9xy=0$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $y^2 = x \implies 2y y' = 1 \implies y' = 1/(2y)$.
- **Example 2**: Circle $x^2+y^2=25$. At $(3, -4)$, slope $dy/dx = -x/y = -3/(-4) = 3/4$.
- **Example 3**: $y^2 = x^2 + \sin xy \implies y' = \frac{2x + y \cos xy}{2y - x \cos xy}$.
- **Example 5**: For the Folium at $(2, 4)$, $dy/dx = 4/5$. Tangent: $y = 4/5 x + 12/5$. Normal: $y = -5/4 x + 13/2$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Hidden Function**

### Panel 1: The Tangled Curve (Implicit vs Explicit)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows Nobita a "Straight Path" $y = x^2$ (Explicit) and a "Tangled Path" $x^3+y^3-9xy=0$ (Implicit). Nobita looks confused by the loop.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! I can't solve for $y$ in this loop! How can I find the slope if I don't have a formula?"
  - **Doraemon**: "You don't need to solve for $y$ first, Nobita! We can differentiate 'implicitly'—finding the slope while the function is still hidden!"

### Panel 2: The $y$ is a Function Rule (The Chain)
- **Visual**: Doraemon uses a magnifying glass on $y^2$. Inside the $y$, we see a tiny $f(x)$ glowing. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Whenever you see $y$, remember it's secretly a function of $x$. So when you differentiate $y^2$, the Chain Rule gives you $2y$ *times* the derivative of the secret function, $dy/dx$!"

### Panel 3: The Circle Shortcut (Example 2)
- **Visual**: Nobita is standing on a giant circle $x^2+y^2=25$ at point $(3,-4)$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Differentiate both sides: $2x + 2y(dy/dx) = 0$. Solving for the slope gives $-x/y$. At your spot, the slope is exactly $3/4$!"
  - **Nobita**: "Wow, no square roots required!"

### Panel 4: The Product Rule Trap (Example 3)
- **Visual**: A glowing term $\sin(xy)$ is on a holographic screen. Doraemon highlights the $xy$ inside.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Be careful with terms like $xy$, Nobita! Since both are functions, you must use the Product Rule: $x(dy/dx) + y(1)$. It's a double-layered secret!"

### Panel 5: The Folium of Descartes (Example 5)
- **Visual**: The beautiful loop of the Folium (Figure 3.32). A glowing Tangent line touches it at $(2,4)$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "This is the Folium! Using our secret technique, we found the tangent slope is $4/5$. It perfectly grazes the loop at $(2,4)$!"

### Panel 6: Tangent and Normal (The Light Ray)
- **Visual**: A light ray hitting a lens (Figure 3.31). Doraemon shows the Tangent line and the Normal line (perpendicular).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The Tangent shows the path, but the Normal shows the direction of the light's hit! Since the tangent slope is $4/5$, the normal is its 'negative reciprocal', $-5/4$. Science and Math, perfectly aligned!"
