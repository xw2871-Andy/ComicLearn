# [Unit 2.5] Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 3.5

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To differentiate the six basic trigonometric functions and apply them to model periodic phenomena like Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM).

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Sine & Cosine Derivatives**:
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$.
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x$.
2. **Derivatives of Other Trig Functions**:
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x) = \sec^2 x$.
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x) = \sec x \tan x$.
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x) = -\csc^2 x$.
   - $\frac{d}{dx}(\csc x) = -\csc x \cot x$.
3. **Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)**:
   - Position: $s = A \cos t$.
   - Velocity: $v = -A \sin t$.
   - Acceleration: $a = -A \cos t$.
4. **Trig Continuity**: Trigonometric functions are continuous wherever they are defined.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 3 (Spring)**: A weight stretched 5 units down: $s = 5 \cos t$. Max speed at rest position ($s=0$), zero speed at endpoints ($s=\pm 5$).
- **Example 5 (Tangent Proof)**: Proving $(\tan x)' = \sec^2 x$ using the Quotient Rule and $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$.
- **Example 6 (Higher Order)**: If $y = \sec x$, then $y'' = \sec^3 x + \sec x \tan^2 x$.

- **Visual**: A circular wheel showing $\sin x \to \cos x \to -\sin x \to -\cos x \to \sin x$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Trig derivatives are a never-ending cycle, Nobita! Sine turns into Cosine, but Cosine turns into its *negative* self!"
  - **Nobita**: "It's like a mathematical carousel!"

### Panel 3: The Bobbing Spring (Example 3)
- **Visual**: A weight hanging from a spring (Figure 3.22) bobbing up and down. Doraemon tracks its position with a holographic cosine wave.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "This is Simple Harmonic Motion. At the top and bottom, the velocity is zero—it's stopping to turn around! But in the middle? That's where it's zooming the fastest!"

### Panel 4: The Tangent Proof (Example 5)
- **Visual**: Doraemon uses the "Quotient Rule Slicer" on $\sin x / \cos x$. The Pythagorean identity $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$ glows as the pieces come together.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Every trig function is just a combination of Sine and Cosine! Using the Quotient Rule, we prove that the slope of Tangent is always a square—the Secant squared!"

### Panel 5: The Secant Tower (Example 6)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at $y = \sec x$. The first derivative is a "two-headed" function $\sec x \tan x$. The second derivative is even more complex.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Higher derivatives of Secant and Tangent get messy fast! It's like building a tower out of smaller trig pieces. $y''$ for secant is $\sec^3 x + \sec x \tan^2 x$!"

### Panel 6: Periodic World
- **Visual**: A montage of heart rhythms, tides, and weather patterns. The trig derivative formulas float above them.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Nature loves periodicity, Nobita. From your heartbeat to the ocean tides, these formulas describe how the rhythms of the world change!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus really is the music of the universe!"
