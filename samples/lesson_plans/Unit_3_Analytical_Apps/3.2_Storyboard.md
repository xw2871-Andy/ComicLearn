# [Unit 3.2] The Mean Value Theorem
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.2

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To understand Rolle's Theorem as a special case and the Mean Value Theorem (MVT) as the general case that connects a function's average rate of change over an interval to its instantaneous rate of change at an interior point.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Rolle's Theorem**: If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, differentiable on $(a, b)$, and $f(a) = f(b)$, then there exists $c \in (a, b)$ where $f'(c) = 0$.
2. **The Mean Value Theorem (MVT)**: If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that:
   $$\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} = f'(c)$$
3. **Physical Interpretation**: The instantaneous change at some interior point equals the average change over the entire interval.
4. **Corollary 1**: If $f'(x) = 0$ on an interval, then $f$ is constant.
5. **Corollary 2**: If $f'(x) = g'(x)$, then $f(x) = g(x) + C$ (functions with identical derivatives differ only by a vertical shift).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Show $x^3+3x+1=0$ has exactly one root using IVT and Rolle's Theorem.
- **Example 2**: For $f(x)=x^2$ on $[0, 2]$, average rate is 2. MVT point is $c=1$ where $f'(1)=2$.
- **Example 3 (Car)**: A car travels 352 ft in 8 sec. Average speed is 44 ft/sec. MVT says at some point the speedometer read exactly 44 ft/sec (30 mph).

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Speedometer Truth**

### Panel 1: The Zero Slope Trap (Rolle's Theorem)
- **Visual**: Nobita is walking on a path that starts and ends at the same height (like a hill that returns to level ground).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Nobita, if you start and end at the same altitude on this smooth hill, there *must* be at least one spot at the top where the path is perfectly flat!"
  - **Nobita**: "So the slope is zero somewhere in the middle? That's Rolle's Theorem!"

### Panel 2: The Parallel Path (MVT)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows a "Secant Cable" connecting two points A and B on a mountain. He uses a "Parallel Laser" to find a spot on the curve with the same tilt.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The Mean Value Theorem is just a tilted version of Rolle's! It says there's a point $c$ where the instantaneous slope matches the average slope of the whole trip."

### Panel 3: The Speeding Ticket (Example 3)
- **Visual**: A police officer (Gian) is looking at a car's travel data. The car went 352 feet in 8 seconds. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Gian**: "I didn't see you speed, but you averaged 44 feet per second!"
  - **Doraemon**: "The MVT proves it, Nobita! Even if the cop didn't see it, your speedometer *must* have hit exactly 44 at some instant!"

### Panel 4: The Zero Derivative Constant (Corollary 1)
- **Visual**: A robot is moving, but its "Change-o-meter" ($f'$) is stuck at zero. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If your rate of change is always zero, you aren't going anywhere, Nobita! You must be a Constant Function. You're stuck in the same place!"

### Panel 5: The Vertical Shift Twins (Corollary 2)
- **Visual**: Two identical parabolas $y=x^2$ and $y=x^2+3$ floating in the air. Their slopes at every $x$ are the same.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Functions with the same derivative are like twins, Nobita! They look exactly the same; one is just sitting a bit higher than the other. They only differ by a Constant $C$."

### Panel 6: The Calculus Backbone
- **Visual**: A summary montage showing the MVT connecting Average Speed (Algebra) and Instantaneous Speed (Calculus).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The MVT is the bridge that lets us recover functions from their rates of change. It's the backbone of everything we'll do next!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus really does connect the dots!"
