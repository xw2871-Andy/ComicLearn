# [Unit 3.3] Monotonic Functions and the First Derivative Test
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.3

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To use the sign of the first derivative to determine where a function is increasing or decreasing (monotonicity) and to apply the First Derivative Test to classify local extrema.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Corollary 3 (Monotonicity)**: 
   - If $f' > 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is increasing on $[a, b]$.
   - If $f' < 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is decreasing on $[a, b]$.
2. **Critical Point Partitioning**: Using critical points to divide the domain into intervals where $f'$ has a constant sign.
3. **First Derivative Test for Local Extrema**:
   - $f'$ changes from $-$ to $+$ $\implies$ local minimum.
   - $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$ $\implies$ local maximum.
   - $f'$ does not change sign $\implies$ no local extremum.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $f(x) = x^3 - 12x - 5$. $f'(x) = 3(x+2)(x-2)$. Critical points at $x = \pm 2$.
  - Increasing on $(-\infty, -2) \cup (2, \infty)$, Decreasing on $(-2, 2)$.
- **Example 2**: $f(x) = x^{1/3}(x-4)$. $f'(x) = \frac{4(x-1)}{3x^{2/3}}$. Critical points at $x=0, 1$.
  - Decreasing on $(-\infty, 1)$, Increasing on $(1, \infty)$.
  - Local minimum at $x=1$, no extremum at $x=0$.
- **Example 3**: $f(x) = \sin^2 x - \sin x - 1$ on $[0, 2\pi]$. Critical points at $\pi/6, 5\pi/6, \pi/2, 3\pi/2$.

**Topic: The Signs of the Climb**

### Panel 1: The Slope Compass (Monotonicity)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows Nobita a "Slope Compass". When it points UP ($f' > 0$), Nobita is climbing. When it points DOWN ($f' < 0$), Nobita is sliding down.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Calculus tells us exactly where the road goes up or down, Nobita! Just look at the sign of the derivative!"

### Panel 2: Partitioning the World (Example 1)
- **Visual**: Nobita is standing on a landscape for $x^3 - 12x - 5$. Doraemon uses "Boundary Flags" at $x=-2$ and $x=2$ to divide the path.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Critical points are our border crossings! Between the flags, the slope can't change its sign. We just need to check one point in each zone!"

### Panel 3: The Peak and Valley Test
- **Visual**: A signpost at a critical point. On the left, Nobita is sliding ($f' < 0$). On the right, he is climbing ($f' > 0$). At the bottom, he finds a "Local Minimum" treasure.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If you're going down and then start going up, you've hit a valley! That's the First Derivative Test in action!"

### Panel 4: The False Alarm (Example 2 at $x=0$)
- **Visual**: Nobita is sliding down a path ($y = x^{1/3}(x-4)$). He passes $x=0$. On both sides of zero, he is still sliding down.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! The derivative was undefined at zero, but I'm still going down! No peak here?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Correct! If the sign doesn't change, there's no extremum. It's just a vertical moment in the slide!"

### Panel 5: The Trig Landscape (Example 3)
- **Visual**: A wavy path made of Sine functions. Multiple flags are placed at $\pi/6, \pi/2, \dots$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Trig functions are like rollercoasters! By solving $f' = 0$, we find every peak and valley in the cycle. At $3\pi/2$, we hit the absolute highest peak of this ride!"

### Panel 6: The Sketcher's Secret
- **Visual**: Doraemon is quickly sketching the graph of a function just by looking at a table of signs ($+$, $-$, $+$).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "With just a few critical points and the First Derivative Test, we can map out the entire behavior of a function without even plotting a hundred points!"
  - **Nobita**: "Calculus gives me a bird's-eye view!"
