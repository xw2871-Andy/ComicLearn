# [Unit 3.6] Newton's Method
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.6

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To use tangent lines to approximate the real roots of equations $f(x)=0$ through the iterative Newton-Raphson method, and to understand the conditions for its convergence.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **The Concept**: Approximate a zero of $f$ with a zero of one of its linearizations.
2. **The Formula**: Given $x_n$, the next approximation is:
   $$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}, \quad f'(x_n) \neq 0$$
3. **Geometry**: From $x_n$, go up to the curve $(x_n, f(x_n))$ and follow the tangent line down to the $x$-axis to find $x_{n+1}$.
4. **Convergence**: Newton's method usually converges with impressive speed, but it can fail if the starting guess is too far away or if the graph has a specific symmetry (Figure 4.51).

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Finding $\sqrt{2}$ by solving $x^2-2=0$. 
  - Formula: $x_{n+1} = x_n/2 + 1/x_n$.
  - With $x_0=1$: $x_1=1.5, x_2=1.41667, x_3=1.41422$ (5 correct digits in 3 steps!).
- **Example 2**: Solving $x^3-x-1=0$. Root is between 1 and 2. 
  - Converges to $1.324717957$ in 5 steps.
- **Failures**: Cases where the method cycles back and forth (Figure 4.51) or jumps to a different root (Figure 4.52).

**Topic: The Tangent Shortcut to Truth**

### Panel 1: The Hidden Root (Introduction)
- **Visual**: Nobita is looking at a high-degree polynomial $x^3-x-1=0$. He's frustrated because there's no simple formula like the quadratic one.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! I can't solve this equation! It's not a square or a line! How do I find where it hits zero?"
  - **Doraemon**: "If you can't solve it exactly, Nobita, we'll use a tangent to hunt it down! It's called Newton's Method!"

### Panel 2: The Tangent Slide (Geometry)
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out a "Tangent Slider". From an initial guess $x_0$, a tangent line slides down the curve and hits the $x$-axis at $x_1$. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "We start with a guess. Then, we follow the curve's 'tilt' down to the ground. That new spot is usually much closer to the real answer!"

### Panel 3: The Root of Two (Example 1)
- **Visual**: A machine labeled "$x_{n+1} = x_n/2 + 1/x_n$". Nobita feeds in $1$. Out pops $1.5$. He feeds in $1.5$, out pops $1.416...$
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Wait, just three steps and I have five digits of the square root of two? This machine is super fast!"

### Panel 4: The Convergence Race (Example 2)
- **Visual**: A table (Table 4.1) glowing in the air. Each step the number of decimal places doubles.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Newton's method is the fastest hunter in math. For $x^3-x-1$, it nails the answer to 9 decimal places in just 5 iterations! Computers love this trick!"

### Panel 5: The Loop of Doom (Failure Case)
- **Visual**: Nobita is on a curve that looks like a shallow "V" (Figure 4.51). He uses the method at $x_0$, it sends him to $x_1$. He uses it at $x_1$, it sends him right back to $x_0$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! I'm stuck in a loop! I'm not getting any closer to the root!"
  - **Doraemon**: "That's a 'Pathological' case, Nobita. If you pick a bad starting spot, the math can spin its wheels forever. Always start with a good guess!"

### Panel 6: The Iteration Master
- **Visual**: Doraemon hands Nobita a "Newton-Raphson Stamp". Nobita stamps it on complex equations, and the roots pop out.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Linearization isn't just for approximation, Nobita. It's an engine for solving the world's hardest equations, one step at a time!"
  - **Nobita**: "I'm not afraid of big equations anymore!"
