# [Unit 3.5] Applied Optimization
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.5

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To use derivatives to find the optimal (maximum or minimum) values for real-world problems in geometry, physics, and economics.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Optimization Strategy**: 1. Understand, 2. Picture, 3. Variables, 4. Equation (Single Variable), 5. Test Critical Points/Endpoints.
2. **Constraint Equations**: Equations used to eliminate one variable in favor of another (e.g., volume constraint for a can).
3. **Economics Rule**: Maximum profit occurs where **Marginal Revenue = Marginal Cost** ($r'(x) = c'(x)$).
4. **Physics (Snell's Law)**: Light follows the path of minimum time. The ratio $\frac{\sin \theta_1}{c_1} = \frac{\sin \theta_2}{c_2}$ minimizes travel time.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1 (Tin Box)**: Cutting squares from a 12x12 sheet. Max volume $128 \text{ in}^3$ occurs when $x=2$.
- **Example 2 (The Can)**: 1-liter cylindrical can. Minimum surface area occurs when height $h$ equals the diameter $2r$.
- **Example 5 (MP3 Players)**: $r(x)=9x, c(x)=x^3-6x^2+15x$. Profit max at $x \approx 3.414$ million units.
- **Example 6 (Inventory Control)**: Minimizing average daily cost $c(x) = \frac{5000}{x} + 25x$ gives a cycle of $\approx 14$ days.

- **Visual**: Nobita is trying to fit a rectangular banner inside a glowing semicircle $y = \sqrt{4-x^2}$.

- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Light is lazy, Nobita! It always takes the path that minimizes time. Nature uses Calculus to solve optimization problems every single day!"
