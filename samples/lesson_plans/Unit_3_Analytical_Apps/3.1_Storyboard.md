# [Unit 3.1] Extreme Values of Functions
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.1

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To identify absolute and local extreme values of functions, understand the conditions of the Extreme Value Theorem (EVT), and use critical points to find extrema on closed intervals.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Absolute Extrema**: The highest ($f(x) \leq f(c)$) or lowest ($f(x) \geq f(c)$) values on the entire domain.
2. **Local Extrema**: The highest or lowest values in an immediate neighborhood (open interval).
3. **Extreme Value Theorem (EVT)**: If $f$ is continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$, then $f$ attains both an absolute maximum $M$ and an absolute minimum $m$.
4. **Critical Point**: An interior point where $f'$ is zero or undefined.
5. **First Derivative Theorem**: If $f$ has a local extremum at an interior point $c$, and $f'(c)$ exists, then $f'(c) = 0$.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $y = x^2$ on $(0, 2)$ has no absolute extrema. On $[0, 2]$, it has both (Min 0 at $x=0$, Max 4 at $x=2$).
- **Example 3**: $g(t) = 8t - t^4$ on $[-2, 1]$. Critical point $t = \sqrt[3]{2}$ is outside $[-2, 1]$. Extrema occur at endpoints: Max 7 at $t=1$, Min $-32$ at $t=-2$.
- **Example 4**: $f(x) = x^{2/3}$ on $[-2, 3]$. $f'(0)$ is undefined. Absolute Min 0 at $x=0$ (cusp). Absolute Max $\sqrt[3]{9}$ at $x=3$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Peak and the Valley**

### Panel 1: The Mountain Range (Extrema)
- **Visual**: Doraemon and Nobita are looking at a mountain range. Nobita points to the highest peak (Absolute Max) and the lowest valley (Absolute Min). 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! I want to find the highest point in this whole range! And the lowest spot too!"
  - **Doraemon**: "In Calculus, we call those Absolute Extrema, Nobita! But we also care about 'Local' peaks—the highest points in just one small neighborhood."

### Panel 2: The Closed Path Rule (EVT)
- **Visual**: A path between two fences (Closed Interval $[a, b]$). The path is smooth and unbroken (Continuous).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The Extreme Value Theorem is like a guarantee. If your path is continuous and has a start and end, you *must* have a highest and lowest point! No excuses!"

### Panel 3: The Critical Point Scanner
- **Visual**: Doraemon pulls out a "Peak Detector". It beeps at two types of spots: 1. Where the slope is zero (flat top), and 2. Where the slope is undefined (sharp point).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "These are Critical Points, Nobita! It's where the derivative is zero or undefined. If there's an interior peak, it *must* be at one of these spots!"

### Panel 4: The Endpoint Trap (Example 3)
- **Visual**: A path for $g(t) = 8t - t^4$ on $[-2, 1]$. The detector beeps far away outside the fence.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! The peak detector found a peak, but it's outside our fences! What do we do?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Then we check the fences themselves! In this interval, the biggest and smallest values are at the very edges—the Endpoints!"

### Panel 5: The Cusp Crater (Example 4)
- **Visual**: A graph of $x^{2/3}$ forming a sharp "V" shape at the origin. Nobita is looking into the sharp point.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Careful! Here at zero, the derivative is undefined because the slope is vertical. But look! It's the absolute lowest point on the graph. Not all extrema are smooth!"

### Panel 6: The Optimization Master
- **Visual**: A montage of a factory maximizing profit, a box with max volume, and blood vessels. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Finding extrema is the secret to 'Optimization'. Whether you're building a spaceship or running a business, you're always looking for the Max and the Min!"
  - **Nobita**: "I'm ready to find the best solutions for everything!"
