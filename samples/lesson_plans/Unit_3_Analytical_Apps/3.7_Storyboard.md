# [Unit 3.7] Antiderivatives
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.7

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To reverse the process of differentiation to find antiderivatives (original functions from their derivatives), solve initial value problems, and introduce the notation of the indefinite integral.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Antiderivative**: A function $F$ such that $F'(x) = f(x)$.
2. **The General Antiderivative**: If $F$ is an antiderivative, then $F(x) + C$ represents all possible antiderivatives (differing by a constant $C$).
3. **Indefinite Integral**: Denoted by $\int f(x) dx$, it is the collection of all antiderivatives of $f$.
4. **Power Rule for Antiderivatives**: $\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C, \quad n \neq -1$.
5. **Initial Value Problem (IVP)**: A differential equation $dy/dx = f(x)$ combined with an initial condition $y(x_0) = y_0$ to find a unique particular solution.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: Antiderivative of $2x$ is $x^2+C$; antiderivative of $\cos x$ is $\sin x+C$.
- **Example 2**: Solve $F'(x)=3x^2$ with $F(1)=-1$. General solution $F(x)=x^3+C \implies 1+C=-1 \implies C=-2 \implies F(x)=x^3-2$.
- **Example 5 (Motion)**: A package dropped from a balloon rising at 12 ft/sec from 80 ft. 
  - Accel $a = -32$. Velocity $v = -32t + 12$. Height $s = -16t^2 + 12t + 80$.
  - Hits ground at $s=0 \implies t \approx 2.64$ sec.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Time Reversal Tool**

### Panel 1: The Time-Reverse Tunnel (Introduction)
- **Visual**: Nobita has a broken vase ($f$). Doraemon pulls out a "Time-Reverse Tunnel" (Antidifferentiation). As the broken pieces pass through, the original vase ($F$) is reconstructed.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "We've learned how to find the rate of change ($f$), Nobita. Now, we're going to reverse time to find the original function ($F$)! This is called an Antiderivative."

### Panel 2: The Constant Ghost (+C)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows three identical vases at different heights. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Be careful! When we reverse time, we can't know the exact starting height. Was it $x^2$? $x^2+1$? Or $x^2-10$? They all have the same derivative! That's why we always add a Constant Ghost: $+C$!"

### Panel 3: The Power Reverse Rule (Table 4.2)
- **Visual**: A machine with a reverse gear. Nobita feeds in $x^2$. The machine adds 1 to the power and divides by the new number. 
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "So instead of subtracting and multiplying, we add and divide? $x^n$ becomes $\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Exactly! Just don't try it when the power is negative one—that's a different secret for later!"

### Panel 4: The Initial Condition Key (Example 2)
- **Visual**: A safe with a lock. To find the specific $C$, Nobita inserts a "Key" labeled $(1, -1)$. The Ghost $+C$ solidifies into $-2$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The $+C$ is a family of curves. But with just one piece of info—an Initial Condition—we can pick the one true path for our specific problem!"

### Panel 5: The Falling Package (Example 5)
- **Visual**: A hot-air balloon with a package falling out. Doraemon tracks the acceleration (-32). As it passes through two reversal tunnels, it becomes Velocity and then Height.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Acceleration is just the second derivative of position! Reverse once for speed, reverse again for height. Calculus tracks the whole flight from just gravity!"

### Panel 6: The Integral Portal
- **Visual**: A giant glowing portal shaped like the Integral sign $\int$. Functions are marching through it.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "This symbol, the Indefinite Integral, is our passport to the second half of Calculus. It's the gateway to Area, Volume, and the Fundamental Theorem!"
  - **Nobita**: "I'm ready for the next big adventure!"
