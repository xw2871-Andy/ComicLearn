# [Unit 3.4] Concavity and Curve Sketching
# Textbook Alignment: Thomas' Calculus 14th Ed, Section 4.4

## Lesson Plan
### Objective
To use the second derivative to determine the concavity of a graph and locate points of inflection, and to integrate all derivative information (increasing/decreasing, extrema, concavity) to sketch accurate graphs of functions.

### Key Concepts (Thomas-Verbatim)
1. **Concavity**: 
   - Concave Up (CU): $f'$ is increasing ($f'' > 0$). Graph holds water.
   - Concave Down (CD): $f'$ is decreasing ($f'' < 0$). Graph sheds water.
2. **Point of Inflection**: A point where the graph has a tangent line and the concavity changes. At this point, $f'' = 0$ or $f''$ is undefined.
3. **Second Derivative Test for Local Extrema**:
   - $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) < 0$ $\implies$ Local Maximum.
   - $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$ $\implies$ Local Minimum.
4. **Graphing Procedure**: 1. Domain/Symmetry, 2. $y', y''$, 3. Critical Points, 4. Intervals of Rise/Fall, 5. Concavity/Inflection, 6. Asymptotes, 7. Plotting.

### Examples (Thomas-Precise)
- **Example 1**: $y = x^3$. CD on $(-\infty, 0)$, CU on $(0, \infty)$. Inflection point at $(0, 0)$.
- **Example 3**: $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2$. Inflection point at $(1, 0)$.
- **Example 5**: $y = x^4$. $y'' = 12x^2$. $y''(0)=0$ but NO inflection point (always CU).
- **Example 8**: Sketching $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3 + 10$.
- **Example 10**: $f(x) = \frac{x^2+4}{2x}$ has an oblique asymptote $y = x/2$ and vertical asymptote $x=0$.

## Storyboard (Doraemon Edition)
**Topic: The Shape of the Smile**

### Panel 1: The Cup and the Umbrella (Concavity)
- **Visual**: Doraemon shows Nobita two gadgets. One is a "Concave Up Cup" (shaped like $x^2$) that catches rain. The other is a "Concave Down Umbrella" (shaped like $-x^2$) that sheds it.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "The second derivative tells us how the path bends, Nobita! If $f'' > 0$, it's a smile (CU). If $f'' < 0$, it's a frown (CD)!"

### Panel 2: The Inflection Point (Example 1)
- **Visual**: Nobita is walking on a path shaped like $y = x^3$. At the origin $(0,0)$, the path changes from a frown to a smile.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Look closely at the origin! The path was shedding water, but now it's catching it. This 'Concavity Flip' is called a Point of Inflection!"

### Panel 3: The Second Derivative Test
- **Visual**: Nobita is at a critical point where the slope is zero. Doraemon uses a "Concavity Probe". It detects a "Smile" ($f'' > 0$).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "If you are at a flat spot and the graph is smiling, you *must* be at the bottom of a valley! That's a Local Minimum!"

### Panel 4: The Fake Inflection (Example 5)
- **Visual**: A graph of $y = x^4$. It looks like a very flat U-shape. $f''(0) = 0$, but there's no concavity change.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Nobita**: "Doraemon! The second derivative is zero, but it's still smiling on both sides! No inflection?"
  - **Doraemon**: "Exactly! To be an inflection point, the concavity *must* flip. Zeros of $f''$ are just candidates!"

### Panel 5: The Full Sketch (Example 10)
- **Visual**: A complex blueprint for $f(x) = \frac{x^2+4}{2x}$. It shows a vertical wall (Asymptote) and a slanted slide (Oblique Asymptote).
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "To draw the whole picture, we combine everything: Critical points for peaks, Second derivatives for bends, and Limits for the edges of the world!"

### Panel 6: The Calculus Artist
- **Visual**: Doraemon hands Nobita a "Mathematical Paintbrush". Nobita sketches a beautiful, accurate curve using a table of $y, y'$, and $y''$.
- **Dialogue**:
  - **Doraemon**: "Now you aren't just guessing, Nobita. You are an artist of logic! You can see the soul of any function just by its derivatives!"
  - **Nobita**: "I can see the curves in my mind now!"
